# Community-Owned Mutual Health Insurance

## Core Idea

A community-owned, vertically-integrated mutual where the risk pool owns the hospital and pays health professionals salaries (not fee-for-service), funded by premium flow. This eliminates profit extraction and volume-based billing fraud.

## Structural Problem It Solves

US health insurance premiums compound at 15-20% annually. Roughly 25% of US health spending is waste or fraud, much of it driven by fee-for-service incentives (over-billing, unnecessary procedures). Job-tied coverage creates coverage gaps and reduces labor mobility.

## How It Works

The employer funds a community pool (not job-tied coverage). The mutual owns the care delivery infrastructure: clinics, hospitals, pharmacy. Health professionals are salaried employees of the mutual, removing the volume-based billing incentive entirely.

Pharmacy must be included or cost-gaming migrates there.

The pool should be broad and semi-mandatory to avoid adverse-selection collapse (only sick people joining, healthy people opting out).

## Membership Tiers

1. **Monthly**: standard recurring premium, predictable for members.
2. **Yearly**: discounted annual payment, improves cash flow for the mutual.
3. **Lifetime (lump sum)**: actuarially, this is a pension/annuity. The lump sum must be invested to cover decades of future liability. This tier carries significant actuarial risk and requires disciplined investment management.

## Key Advantages

- **No profit extraction**: mutual is member-owned, surplus returns to care or reserves.
- **No volume-billing fraud**: salaried providers have no incentive to over-treat.
- **Low admin overhead**: single-payer-style administration (~2% admin cost vs 15-25% for commercial insurers).
- **Radical transparency**: members are owners, financials are open.
- **Employer-funded community pool**: decouples coverage from employment, improving labor mobility.

## Precedents

- **Mutual insurers / friendly societies**: member-owned risk pools with long historical track records.
- **Kaiser Permanente**: integrated care delivery (insurer owns hospitals), though Kaiser uses a mix of salary and capitation, not pure salary.
- **NHS GP model**: salaried general practitioners, no fee-for-service incentive.
- **Single-payer admin benchmarks**: Medicare admin ~2% of spend vs commercial 15-25%.

## Novelty

The combination of mutual ownership + salaried integrated care + radical transparency. Each element exists in precedents; the specific combination does not.

## Open Problems

1. **Adverse selection**: if membership is voluntary, healthy individuals may opt out, leaving the pool with disproportionately sick (expensive) members. Broad, semi-mandatory enrollment is the mitigation.
2. **Catastrophic / out-of-network care**: the mutual cannot own every specialty or handle every rare condition in-house. Reinsurance contracts are necessary for catastrophic events and referrals outside the network.
3. **Under-treatment from salaried staff**: removing the over-treatment incentive could flip to under-treatment. Governance, transparency, and outcome-based quality metrics are the counterbalance.
4. **Geographic concentration**: a single mutual serves a local area. To scale, federate local mutuals under a shared reinsurance umbrella, preserving local governance while pooling catastrophic risk.
5. **Lifetime-tier actuarial discipline**: the lump-sum tier is effectively a pension obligation. Requires conservative investment assumptions, regular actuarial review, and possibly regulatory oversight to prevent underfunding.
